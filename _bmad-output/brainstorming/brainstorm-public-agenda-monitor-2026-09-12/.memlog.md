- (direction by user) Team of four women has two half-days to build a hackathon prototype.
- (idea by user) A documentation/editorial service provides a graphical agenda list to documentary researchers and journalists; documentary researchers remain the human relevance and correctness reviewers.
- (idea by user) The service maintains a daily updated two-week forward-looking event list, highlighting new entries and supporting filters for new events, date, event, and event type plus add/remove curation actions.
- (idea by user) The event record includes date, optional time, event/short description, event type, source name, direct source link, and later an update status; the table must be extendable.
- (direction by user) In scope: regularly scan public sites/feeds daily or twice daily and add high-value information; out of scope: Reuters/DPA subscriptions and Facebook/Instagram content.
- (direction by user) Initial public sources: Federal Constitutional Court, Federal Administrative Court, UN, ship trackers, Bundespräsident.de and Destatis. Relevance examples include court case starts/judgments rather than ordinary hearing days, all presidential appointments, and the consumer price index.
- (technique) started Job to Be Done
- (idea by coach) Potential underlying job: reduce the editorial risk of missing a story-changing public event, rather than merely presenting more events.
- (decision by user) Use the first three selected techniques only: Job to Be Done, Role Playing, and One Feature Only; skip Disney Method.
- (idea by user) Editors should find all potentially important events in one list; their news value still needs definition.
- (idea by user) Documentary researchers approve list entries before editors see them.
- (idea by user) Events are found automatically through criteria-driven web scraping and source/feed searches instead of manual research.
- (insight by coach) Proposed workflow separation: a candidate queue for automated finds and a separate editorial agenda containing only documentary-researcher-approved events.
- (decision by user) Start with a narrow source/rule corpus rather than a generic news-value score.
- (idea by user) Federal Administrative Court: include every case start and every judgment announcement.
- (idea by user) Federal President: include every appointment.
- (idea by user) UN Women: include high-level panels, ministerial meetings, and UN reports on gender equality, violence, care work, and gender data.
- (insight by coach) Initial news value can be rule-based and auditable: defined source plus defined event class equals a candidate worthy of documentary-researcher review.
- (decision by user) Use a configurable 1–5 news-value score for test purposes; the team must be able to adjust it later to optimize Monitor results.
- (direction by user) Scoring lens: potential relevance to a respected independent news program in Germany.
- (direction by user) Federal Administrative Court and Federal President sources can use rule-based qualification; UN Women requires freer editorial scoring.
- (insight by coach) Recommended design: keep inclusion rules, a suggested 1–5 score, score reasons, and a documentary-researcher override as separate editable fields.
- (idea by coach) Initial editable score heuristic: 5 = likely agenda-setting national/international development; 4 = concrete decision, judgment, high-level meeting or major report; 3 = editorially plausible scheduled event; 2 = specialist/limited relevance; 1 = low likely news relevance. Suggested defaults: Federal Administrative Court judgment 4, case start 3; Federal President appointment 3 before title-based adjustment; UN Women high-level/ministry event 4 and report/panel 3–5 based on subject and novelty.
- (decision by user) Defer detailed scoring; initially use the system’s own heuristic, but prioritize listing every event with potential news value for the client.
- (insight by coach) MVP ranking principle: optimize recall first—include plausible candidates even when uncertain; use the score later for ordering and editorial refinement rather than as a hard exclusion gate.
- (technique) started Role Playing
- (idea by user) New events are visually highlighted for documentary researchers.
- (idea by user) Documentary researchers can sort/filter new events and create their own ranking order.
- (idea by coach) Proposed review view: a “new since last review” queue with a manual priority order that is independent from the automatic suggested score.
- (idea by user) Documentary researchers can approve or reject an event, add a comment, and change its news score.
- (direction by user) Score corrections should train/improve the Public Agenda Monitor over time to reflect what matters to the editorial team.
- (insight by coach) Every review should retain the original suggested score, the human-adjusted score, decision, and comment as an auditable feedback record for later optimization.
- (idea by user) The approved editor agenda must show date, event name/topic, protagonists or organizer, location, source, and direct link.
- (insight by coach) The editor view is read-focused: a concise verified agenda record plus direct source access, separate from documentary-researcher review controls.
- (technique) started One Feature Only
- (decision by user) Hackathon MVP is one complete end-to-end flow: the system finds events from one source, a documentary researcher reviews them, and editors see the approved agenda.
- (insight by coach) The MVP success story is an auditable chain from source link to candidate, human decision, and editor-visible record—not broad multi-source scraping.
- (idea by coach) All three sources are feasible in the hackathon if each is a narrow adapter: retrieve only its public page/feed, apply explicit event-type rules, and transform results into one shared event schema; avoid a generic crawler and login-protected sources.
- (decision by user) MVP must include all three sources: Federal Administrative Court, Federal President, and UN Women, subject to the narrow public-source adapter constraints.
- (decision by user) Apply the pre-mortem countermeasures. Build and demonstrate one complete source-to-editor vertical slice first; add the other sources only after it succeeds, using an extensible adapter design.
- (direction by user) MVP guardrails: fixed public page/feed per source, simple editable qualification rules, persistent review fields, fallback test data, and a demo narrative from source to approved agenda.
- (insight) Synthesis: The product is a human-curated editorial agenda, not a generic news scraper. Its defining chain is public source -> high-recall candidate -> documentary-researcher decision with feedback -> editor-visible verified agenda. The hackathon proof is a one-source vertical slice protected by explicit data, scope, and demo guardrails, with adapters enabling later expansion to all three sources.
